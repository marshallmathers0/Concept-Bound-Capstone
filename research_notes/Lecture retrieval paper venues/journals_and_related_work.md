## (a) Which rolling-submission journals are realistic, and what do they cost, how fast are they, and how are they indexed?

### Takeaway
The realistic primary targets are **Education and Information Technologies (Springer, hybrid, free subscription route, fast first decision)**, **IEEE Access (fast, but USD 2,160 APC with no student discount)** and **PeerJ Computer Science (OA, APC about USD 1,888 per PeerJ's own page, with country waivers)**. **Multimedia Tools and Applications** is a free hybrid fallback, but its reputation should be checked. **Smart Learning Environments**, **Computers and Education: AI** and **IEEE TLT** have high impact factors and are stretch targets for a 30-video single-institution study. Of the data journals, **Data in Brief** (about USD 530, has a waiver policy) is a far better fit than **Scientific Data**. Both require the data to be openly deposited, which clashes with releasing classroom video that contains identifiable students and faculty.

### Inferences
- **Suggested ranking for this paper (an applied student study, about 30 videos, one institution):**
  1. **Education and Information Technologies**: free route, broad ed-tech scope that welcomes system and evaluation papers, fast desk decision. Realistic if the paper reports a learner-facing evaluation, not only IR metrics. Moderate chance.
  2. **PeerJ Computer Science**: judges technical soundness rather than novelty (PeerJ's general policy, not re-verified here), and is OA. Realistic if code and annotations are released. Moderate-to-good chance, subject to APC/waiver.
  3. **IEEE Access**: realistic acceptance for a sound applied study, but at about INR 1.8 lakh with no student discount it is expensive. Its reputation is "acceptable but megajournal". Use it only if the college pays.
  4. **MTAP**: free, and its scope covers multimodal retrieval. A reasonable fallback. Check the current WoS status and recent retraction/quality news first (not verified this session).
  5. **Stretch targets**: IEEE TLT, which has strong precedent with Yang and Meinel 2014 but would expect learning outcomes or a larger corpus. SLE (IF about 12, no waiver) and CAEAI (IF about 23 per an aggregator) both expect educational impact evidence. Low chance unless the paper adds a student user study.
  6. **IJETHE**: its focus is higher-education research and policy. A technical retrieval paper is a weak scope fit, so a desk rejection is likely despite the possible zero APC.
- **Data release**: Data in Brief is the right choice if the team can openly deposit the annotations (query-to-timestamp labels, segment boundaries, transcripts or features). Scientific Data's bar (a reusable, substantial dataset) and its APC make it a poor fit for 30 hours from one college. **Consent and privacy for classroom video (students' faces and voices) must be resolved before any data journal**. A release of "annotations + ASR transcripts + video IDs on request" may be the only feasible one.
- On arXiv, the team needs a faculty endorser who already has arXiv CS papers, since an institutional email alone no longer works.

## (b) What is the closest related work, and what gap can a 2027 paper on Indian classroom lecture-moment retrieval claim?

### Takeaway
Lecture segmentation (AVLectures, CLVTS, VT-SSum) and slide-centred lecture datasets (LPM, M3AV, Video-MMLU) are well covered. General moment retrieval benchmarks (TVR, QVHighlights, Ego4D NLQ, MAD) do not cover lectures. The closest direct competitors are recent and weak or unstable: **CourseTimeQA (arXiv Dec 2025) was withdrawn in June 2026 over a measurement error**, and LectQA-Vid (CMC 2026) is a small-journal benchmark. Indian NPTEL resources are speech-only or have small linking sets. The defensible gap is **a human-annotated query-to-moment benchmark of real single-camera Indian classroom recordings, with an honest comparison against strong text-only baselines (BM25, dense, cross-encoder over Whisper transcripts)**.

### Cited Findings

**Lecture video segmentation**
- **AVLectures / Unsupervised Audio-Visual Lecture Segmentation** (WACV 2023, IIIT Hyderabad). Introduces 86 courses and more than 2,350 lectures (STEM). Segmentation is unsupervised, using visual, text and OCR cues with TW-FINCH clustering. It is the standard lecture segmentation benchmark, with a 350-lecture annotated subset. *Differ:* that corpus is MIT-OCW-style recorded content. This paper uses a real Indian single-camera classroom and adds a trained boundary detector judged against retrieval utility — [arXiv 2210.16644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.16644); [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2023/papers/S._Unsupervised_Audio-Visual_Lecture_Segmentation_WACV_2023_paper.pdf); [CausalChapter usage](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08686)
- **Multimodal Fusion and Coherence Modeling for Video Topic Segmentation / CLVTS** (arXiv Aug 2024). A Chinese lecture dataset with longer uninterrupted videos. It notes that about two-thirds of AVLectures are reassembled pre-segmented short videos, so AVLectures boundaries are partly artificial. *Differ:* this is the strongest argument that genuine continuous one-hour classroom recordings are under-served — [arXiv 2408.00365](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.00365)
- **VT-SSum** (CIKM 2021, Microsoft). 9,616 VideoLectures.NET videos and 125K transcript-summary pairs. Slides act as weak supervision for segmentation and summarization. *Differ:* its segment labels are slide-derived, not human concept boundaries, and it has no query retrieval — [arXiv/ar5iv 2106.05606](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.05606); [GitHub](https://github.com/Dod-o/VT-SSum)
- **From Text Segmentation to Smart Chaptering** (arXiv Feb 2024). A transcript-chaptering benchmark. It criticizes earlier lecture transcript segmentation work as single-domain with slide-constructed segments — [arXiv 2402.17633](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17633)
- **NPTEL lecture segmentation with slide and transcript cues** (arXiv 2012.07589). Segments roughly one-hour NPTEL lectures. *Differ:* NPTEL is studio-recorded with slides, while this paper uses an ordinary classroom camera — [arXiv 2012.07589](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.07589)
- **LLM-era chaptering**: CausalChapter (arXiv 2609.08686) and "Towards Multi-Level Transcript Segmentation: LoRA Fine-Tuning for ToC Generation" (arXiv 2601.02128). These are the LLM baselines a 2027 segmentation section should compare against or cite — [CausalChapter](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08686); [2601.02128](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02128)
- **TextTiling** (Hearst, Computational Linguistics 1997). The classic lexical-cohesion segmentation baseline that should be included. URL from prior knowledge, not fetched: https://aclanthology.org/J97-1003/

**Lecture video retrieval and search systems**
- **Yang and Meinel, "Content Based Lecture Video Retrieval Using Speech and Video Text Information"** (IEEE TLT 2014, DOI 10.1109/TLT.2014.2307305). ASR plus OCR indexing of lecture videos (tele-TASK). This is the direct ancestor of the ASR+OCR pipeline. *Differ:* this paper adds neural dense and cross-modal retrieval and moment-level human evaluation — [HPI BibTeX](https://hpi.de/oldsite/fileadmin/user_upload/fachgebiete/meinel/tele-task/papers/2014_Yang_TLT.txt)
- **A Cross-media Retrieval System for Lecture Videos** (arXiv cs/0309021, 2003). Early speech and slide retrieval — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0309021)
- **Ghosh et al., "Augmenting Video Lectures: Identifying Off-topic Concepts and Linking to Relevant Video Lecture Segments"** (Int. J. AI in Education, Springer). Retrieves and ranks NPTEL video segments for concepts, evaluated on 6 manually curated NPTEL courses, with code and data on GitHub. **This is the closest Indian prior work and must be cited.** *Differ:* its queries are concepts, not free-text student questions, and its videos are NPTEL studio recordings, not classroom capture — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-021-00257-z)
- **CourseTimeQA** (Kovalev and Kumar, arXiv 2512.00360, Nov 2025). Timestamped QA over lectures with 52.3 h, 902 queries and 6 courses, with BLIP-2, CLIP and cross-encoder baselines. **v2 (2 Jun 2026) was withdrawn because "an error in our measurement procedure produced incorrect values"**. The video source is unspecified and no data release is stated. *Differ:* cite it carefully as withdrawn. It shows the task is current but that no reliable public benchmark exists — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00360)
- **Temporally aware intra-video RAG / LectQA-Vid** (Computers, Materials & Continua, 2026). 100 lecture videos and 3,000 temporally annotated questions. Retrieval is constrained to timestamped ASR and caption segments — [TechScience](https://www.techscience.com/cmc/online/detail/27072/pdf) (details from search snippet; video provenance not checked)
- **ALIVE** (arXiv 2512.20858, Dec 2025). Avatar-lecture engine with content-aware, timestamp-aligned retrieval on local hardware, demonstrated on a medical-imaging course — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.20858)

**Lecture video datasets**
- **LectureVideoDB** (Dutta, Mathew, Krishnan, Jawahar; ICFHR 2018, IIIT Hyderabad). More than 5,000 lecture frames, including blackboard and paper writing, annotated for text detection and recognition. Existing methods performed poorly on it. *Use:* to justify the OCR difficulty on classroom boards — [CVIT project page](https://cvit.iiit.ac.in/research/projects/cvit-projects/lecturevideodb); [paper PDF](https://cdn.iiit.ac.in/cdn/cvit.iiit.ac.in/images/ConferencePapers/2018/localizing-recognizing-text.pdf)
- **LPM, Lecture Presentations Multimodal Dataset** (Lee et al., ICCV 2023, CMU). More than 180 h and 9,000 slides from 10 lecturers. Tasks are figure-to-text and text-to-figure retrieval and slide explanation, with the PolyViLT model. Licence CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (per mirror). *Differ:* it is slide-centric with no temporal query localization — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/Lee_Lecture_Presentations_Multimodal_Dataset_Towards_Understanding_Multimodality_in_Educational_Videos_ICCV_2023_paper.html); [arXiv 2208.08080](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.08080)
- **M3AV** (arXiv 2403.14168). Academic lecture dataset combining slide, speech and paper, with human annotation and a slide/script generation task. I could not confirm the ACL 2024 venue — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.14168v3)
- **Video-MMLU** (arXiv 2504.14693, 2025). 1,065 multi-discipline lecture videos used for captioning and quiz QA with multimodal LLMs. It does not cover segmentation or moment retrieval. (The "MMLU-free" item in the brief probably means this one.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14693v2)
- **LectureBank** (Li, Fabbri, Tung, Radev; AAAI 2019). 1,352 lecture files and 208 labeled prerequisite pairs for NLP education. *Use:* only tangential, for concept and prerequisite structure — [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/4638); [arXiv 1811.12181](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.12181)
- **Indian-English lecture speech**: AI4Bharat NPTEL2020 (about 15,700 h, transcripts not manually checked) and TIE (about 9.8K NPTEL lectures with transcripts). *Use:* ASR adaptation or WER evaluation for Indian English. Neither has segment or retrieval labels — [AI4Bharat GitHub](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/NPTEL2020-Indian-English-Speech-Dataset); [TIE GitHub](https://github.com/raianand1991/TIE)

**Video moment retrieval and video corpus moment retrieval**
- **QVHighlights / Moment-DETR** (Lei et al., NeurIPS 2021). More than 10K YouTube videos (vlogs and news) with free-form queries, relevant moments and saliency — [NeurIPS PDF](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/62e0973455fd26eb03e91d5741a4a3bb-Paper.pdf); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=tfBBt_q4nHT)
- **TVR** (Lei et al., ECCV 2020, video corpus moment retrieval on TV shows) and **TVR-Ranking** (arXiv 2407.06597, 2024, ranked moment retrieval with imprecise queries). *Differ:* lecture queries are conceptual and evidence is mostly speech and board text, not visual events — [TVR-Ranking](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.06597); TVR arXiv 2001.09099 (from prior knowledge, not fetched)
- **Event-aware Video Corpus Moment Retrieval** (arXiv 2402.13566) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13566)
- **Recent moment retrieval benchmarks (2026)**: "Retrieving Any Relevant Moments" (arXiv 2605.02623), "Beyond Caption-Based Queries" (arXiv 2603.02363), and "Beyond Closed-Pool Video Retrieval" (arXiv 2602.10159). Only reference lists were seen, so domains are unverified — [2605.02623](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02623); [2603.02363](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.02363); [2602.10159](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10159)
- **Instructional-video answer localization**: **TutorialVQA** (arXiv 1912.01046, about 6,000 video/question/answer-span triples over software screencasts) and **MedVidQA** (3,010 questions over 899 medical videos, with corpus-level MedVidCQA). The VPTSL method predicts subtitle spans, so a text-only method beats visual methods by a large margin. **This is the closest task formulation and strong evidence that transcript-span methods are a must-run baseline** — [TutorialVQA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.01046v1); [MedVidQA/VPTSL arXiv 2203.06667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06667v6); [corpus VAL arXiv 2210.05423](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.05423); [IEEE version](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10552074)
- **Ego4D NLQ** (Grauman et al., CVPR 2022) and **MAD** (Soldan et al., CVPR 2022, long-form movie grounding). Both are long-video grounding benchmarks to cite for the "hour-long video" framing. arXiv IDs from prior knowledge, not fetched: Ego4D 2110.07058, MAD 2112.00431.

**Dense and LLM retrieval baselines (from prior knowledge; arXiv IDs not re-fetched this session)**
- BM25 is the lexical baseline. Sentence-BERT (EMNLP 2019, arXiv 1908.10084) is already used.
- **E5** (Wang et al. 2022, arXiv 2212.03533) and **BGE / C-Pack** (Xiao et al., arXiv 2309.07597, SIGIR 2024). BGE-M3 (arXiv 2402.03216) adds hybrid dense, sparse and multi-vector retrieval.
- **ColBERT** (Khattab and Zaharia, SIGIR 2020, arXiv 2004.12832) and ColBERTv2 (NAACL 2022, arXiv 2112.01488) for late interaction.
- **Cross-encoder rerankers**: monoBERT (Nogueira and Cho 2019, arXiv 1901.04085) and BGE-reranker. Also BEIR (NeurIPS 2021 Datasets, arXiv 2104.08663) as the evaluation precedent showing that BM25 is hard to beat out of domain.
- The CourseTimeQA baselines (BLIP-2, CLIP with cross-encoder, text-only hybrid with cross-encoder) show what reviewers now expect — [arXiv 2512.00360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00360)

### Inferences
- **The genuine gap to claim:** (1) No public, human-annotated benchmark of **free-text student queries mapped to timestamped moments in real single-camera Indian classroom lectures**. Existing lecture data is MIT-OCW or VideoLectures.NET (AVLectures, VT-SSum), slide-centric (LPM, M3AV), Chinese (CLVTS), speech-only Indian data (NPTEL2020, TIE), or concept-linking on studio NPTEL (Ghosh et al.). (2) The only directly comparable 2025 lecture timestamped-QA benchmark (CourseTimeQA) was withdrawn, and LectQA-Vid is unverified in provenance. (3) Real classroom conditions are under-studied: Indian-English ASR errors, board writing and OCR difficulty (LectureVideoDB shows this), no slide transitions to segment on, and continuous one-hour sessions (CLVTS's critique of AVLectures).
- **Honest-empirical framing**: MedVidQA/VPTSL suggests transcript-span text retrieval often dominates visual signals in instructional video. The paper should ask whether the CLIP/OCR fusion and trained boundary detector add anything over BM25 + BGE/E5 + cross-encoder on Whisper transcripts with fixed windows. A negative or mixed result, honestly reported with ablations, is publishable in EAIT or PeerJ CS.
- Report moment metrics (R@1 at IoU 0.3/0.5/0.7, mIoU, as in QVHighlights and MedVidQA) and ranking metrics (MRR, nDCG@10), plus inter-annotator agreement for boundaries and query labels.
- Scale is the main weakness (about 30 h, one institution). Position it as a challenge or test set, not a training set, and evaluate zero-shot strong baselines.
