# Bet on January deadlines, hold April floors

The paper won't be ready until mid-January 2027, and by then most of the 2026 cycle has closed. Almost every strong Indian venue has shut for this cycle. The realistic plan is a ladder with three rungs. The **realistic international target is IEEE ICALT 2027** (verified deadline **15 Jan 2027**, 92 days away, IEEE Xplore with Scopus/EI submission). The **stretch is the ACM ICMR 2027 short paper** (verified deadline **31 Jan 2027**, 108 days away, ACM DL). The **safe Indian floor is IEEE INDISCON 2027** (estimated deadline around **30 Apr 2027**, 197 days away), with IEEE ICCUBEA 2027 and CVIP 2027 Round 1 as alternates. ICALT and ICMR review the same paper in overlapping windows, so dual-submission rules mean the team can send the manuscript to only one of them. The default is ICALT. Switch to ICMR only if a go/no-go check on 5 Jan 2027 shows a sharp, gold-set-backed result. Whatever happens at that venue, the Indian floor follows its notification (1 Apr or 12 Apr). A stronger problem than the venue choice is that the prototype cannot be published yet. The OCR modality is empty, both stages are scored against circular pseudo-labels, and there is no human gold set. So the calendar below spends almost all of October–December on consent, annotation and re-running experiments, and leaves writing for the first two weeks of January. The best claim available is not multimodal novelty. It is the first human-annotated query-to-moment benchmark of real single-camera Indian classroom lectures, with an honest test against strong text baselines. The closest competing benchmark, CourseTimeQA, was withdrawn in June 2026 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00360)).

## Three targets form a ladder, not a parallel bet

The three primary targets cannot all receive the same manuscript at once. ICALT reviews from 15 Jan to 12 Apr and ICMR from 31 Jan to 1 Apr, so submitting to both would be a dual submission. Both venues are also double-blind ([ICALT](https://tc.computer.org/tclt/icalt-2027/); [ICMR](https://www.icmr2027.org/cfc)), so the paper must not name the college. Call the dataset "classroom lectures from an Indian engineering college" until the camera-ready.

**1. Realistic international, the default opener: IEEE ICALT 2027, 15 Jan 2027.** It has a verified date, an indexed IEEE publisher, a tolerant applied-systems culture, and a notification on 12 Apr that falls inside the academic year. The 5-page limit includes references, so the paper must be trimmed to the core: the system, the gold benchmark, and one decisive ablation comparing concept segments against fixed windows, run against BM25, dense and cross-encoder baselines.

**2. Stretch, which replaces ICALT only if the gate passes: ACM ICMR 2027 short paper, 31 Jan 2027.** Pick ICMR on 5 Jan only if three conditions hold. The gold set must be frozen with inter-annotator agreement reported. The 4-page paper must state one clear benchmark finding, such as "strong text baselines match or beat multimodal fusion on real classroom video" or the reverse. And the team must judge that the extra 16 days produce a better paper rather than a rescue. An ICMR rejection on 1 Apr still leaves the full April ladder: **AIED LBR (~5 Apr) → ICCUBEA (~10 Apr) or INDISCON (~30 Apr)**.

**3. Safe Indian floor: IEEE INDISCON 2027, ~30 Apr 2027.** It is the only floor that falls after both possible openers' notifications. **CVIP 2027 Round 1** (Springer, mid/late April, estimated) is the more reputable alternate if a June notification still counts for marks. **ICCUBEA 2027** (~10 Apr, about 4-week review) is the fastest floor, but only on the ICMR path.

The backups follow the same timing logic. **AIED 2027 LBR** and **EDM posters** in early April are the international catch-nets after an ICMR rejection. **L@S WiP** (~16 Feb) is the opener to use instead of ICMR if the education framing is stronger than the retrieval framing. **CBMI** and **ICME workshops** in April suit a retrieval framing.

One more rule matters if the department needs an acceptance letter before mid-February. Then **CONECCT (15 Dec, notification 15 Feb)** or **I2ITCON (~mid-Jan, estimated)** is the only route that works in time. Taking it closes ICALT and ICMR for this manuscript, apart from a later journal extension with substantial new content.

## The calendar runs backward from 15 January

Three blockers drive the schedule, and none of them is writing. First, the **OCR modality is empty in every video**, so any "multimodal" claim is unsupported until OCR produces text. LectureVideoDB already shows that text recognition on classroom boards is hard ([CVIT](https://cvit.iiit.ac.in/research/projects/cvit-projects/lecturevideodb)). Second, **both stages are scored against circular pseudo-labels**, meaning the system is evaluated against labels it generated, so every current number is uninterpretable. Third, **there is no human gold set**.

Fixing these means annotating concept boundaries, collecting 300–500 real student queries mapped to timestamps, and double-annotating a subset (about 20–25% is a sensible target) to report agreement. All of this runs alongside growing the corpus from 11 to about 30 videos and alongside end-semester exams.

Consent comes first, because without it nothing else can be released. The recordings show identifiable instructors and students, so the team needs three things in writing before anything else:

- institutional permission from the college, which likely owns or controls the recordings
- consent from each instructor, covering the lecture content and their likeness
- a decision on how student faces and voices are handled

Ask the college whether its ethics committee should review the study, and what India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 requires. Resource-track reviewers expect licensing and release clarity. Data journals require open deposit ([Scientific Data FAQ](https://www.nature.com/sdata/faq)). If full video release is impossible, the fallback release is annotations, ASR transcripts, features and video IDs available on request. State that limitation openly in the paper.

## The defensible gap is real classroom audio, not multimodal novelty

The lecture-video literature is crowded, so the paper must claim a narrow, true gap. Segmentation is well covered by three lines of work:

- **AVLectures** (WACV 2023): more than 2,350 lectures with unsupervised audio-visual segmentation ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.16644))
- **CLVTS**: notes that about two-thirds of AVLectures consists of reassembled short videos, which makes its boundaries partly artificial ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.00365))
- **VT-SSum**: slide-derived labels over 9,616 videos ([GitHub](https://github.com/Dod-o/VT-SSum))

Slide-centred datasets such as LPM (ICCV 2023, more than 180 hours) do not localize queries in time ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/Lee_Lecture_Presentations_Multimodal_Dataset_Towards_Understanding_Multimodality_in_Educational_Videos_ICCV_2023_paper.html)). General moment-retrieval benchmarks such as QVHighlights cover vlogs and news, not lectures ([NeurIPS](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/62e0973455fd26eb03e91d5741a4a3bb-Paper.pdf)).

Three prior works must be cited and distinguished from this one:

- **Yang and Meinel**, in IEEE TLT 2014, built the ancestor ASR+OCR lecture search system ([HPI](https://hpi.de/oldsite/fileadmin/user_upload/fachgebiete/meinel/tele-task/papers/2014_Yang_TLT.txt)).
- **Ghosh et al.** is the closest Indian work. It links concepts to NPTEL video segments, but it uses concept queries rather than free-text student questions, and NPTEL studio recordings rather than classroom capture ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-021-00257-z)).
- **CourseTimeQA** built 902 timestamped queries over 52.3 hours of lectures. It was withdrawn after "an error in our measurement procedure" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00360)), so no reliable public lecture moment benchmark exists.

Indian lecture resources such as AI4Bharat's NPTEL2020 are speech-only, with no segment or retrieval labels ([GitHub](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/NPTEL2020-Indian-English-Speech-Dataset)).

The claim to make is that this is **the first human-annotated benchmark mapping free-text student queries to timestamped moments in continuous, single-camera Indian classroom lectures**. The setting has specific difficulties: Indian-English ASR errors, board writing instead of slides, and hour-long sessions with no slide transitions to segment on. The paper should also deliver **an honest test of whether concept segmentation and visual/OCR fusion add anything over strong text retrieval**.

That framing turns a likely result into a contribution. In MedVidQA, a method that predicts subtitle spans beat visual methods by a large margin ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06667v6)). If text baselines win here too, that is a finding, not a failure.

Present the roughly 30-hour corpus as a zero-shot **test set**, not a training set. Report moment metrics (R@1 at IoU 0.3/0.5/0.7 and mIoU) and ranking metrics (MRR and nDCG@10), and report inter-annotator agreement for both boundaries and query labels. Before submission, run a final novelty search on Google Scholar and the ACL Anthology for non-NPTEL Indian classroom retrieval, because that search has not been done exhaustively.

## Verify indexing at the source; ignore WASET look-alikes

Lecture-video work attracts fake conferences with near-identical names. **WASET's "ICCVIP 2027"** runs the same title in Zurich, Dubai, London, Tokyo and Singapore. Its listings show notification dates earlier than submission deadlines, and it is not India's CVIP ([conferenceindex](https://conferenceindex.org/event/international-conference-on-computer-vision-and-image-processing-iccvip-2027-january-zurich-ch)). The **"ICCE 2027" listings in New York, Manila and Barcelona** are also WASET events, not APSCE's ICCE ([conferenceindex](https://conferenceindex.org/event/international-conference-on-computers-in-education-icce-2027-april-new-york-us)). ENNS has documented WASET spoofing real conference names to collect fees ([ENNS](https://e-nns.org/icann2018/index.php/2016/11/11/beware-of-fake-conferences)). Treat first-edition events whose submission link says "Update Soon", such as ICMDRSE 2027 ([VU Pune](https://icmdrse.vupune.ac.in/callpaper.php)), and dormant series such as ICACCI as unverified.

The check takes ten minutes:

- Confirm the IEEE conference record number on conferences.ieee.org.
- Open the previous edition on IEEE Xplore, Springer Link or the ACM DL.
- Confirm the series' Scopus coverage reads "to Present" at scopus.com/sources ([AIScholar](https://www.aischolar.com/news/article/how-to-verify-scopus-indexing)).
- Check that the committee members are real IIT, NIT, IISc, ISI or IIIT faculty.

Legitimate venues take **about 2–9 weeks from deadline to notification** (INDICON about 9 weeks, CONECCT about 9, ICCUBEA about 4, I2ITCON about 2) ([IEEE India Council](https://ieeeindiacouncil.org/ieee-india-council-international-conference-indicon-2026/); [PCCOE](https://www.research.pccoepune.com/conferences.html)). Distrust any venue that accepts within days, promises "Scopus journal" publication from a conference, or presses for early-bird fees ([Exordo](https://www.exordo.com/blog/9-signs-this-is-a-fake-conference/)).

For marks, remember that IEEE-section conferences put papers in Xplore only after **in-person presentation and post-conference review** ([ICCUBEA](https://iccubea.pccoepune.com/authors-guidelines.php)). So the evidence the department actually receives is the acceptance letter plus the presentation certificate.

## Conclusion

The binding constraint is not where to publish but whether there is anything trustworthy to publish by 5 January. A paper scored against its own pseudo-labels, with an empty OCR channel, would be rejected everywhere on this list except the venues that should be avoided anyway. Seen that way, the deadlines that "passed" this cycle are no loss, because the 2026 Indian venues would have received an unpublishable prototype. The schedule's real decision is the 5 Jan gate, which trades ICALT's odds against ICMR's prestige.

The most valuable output of this project may outlast this paper. A consented, double-annotated classroom benchmark with documented agreement is publishable as a resource for years: at T4E 2027, CIKM Resource, ECIR Resource 2028 or Data in Brief. An honest finding that text baselines are hard to beat on real Indian classrooms is exactly what the withdrawn CourseTimeQA failed to deliver reliably. Spending October and November on consent and annotation rather than on model tweaks is what makes either outcome possible.
